# Hiring manager idea email

One short email that gets an interview because they already understand the team's real problem. Use outreach for contacts and its cold-email bar: short, specific, one ask.

## 0. Decide if this company deserves it
All five must hold.
1. **Strong fit.** A role they truly want, with proof that maps to it.
2. **Small enough to be read.** Usually series A to C, where a technical leader, a founder, or a named leader reads cold email. Skip big brands with heavy screening.
3. **Public material.** Blogs, case studies, or webinars that describe customer problems in the company's own words.
4. **A real problem** their background speaks to.
5. **A person you can reach, with a reason.** The hiring manager, someone who invited outreach, or the recruiter, with an email you can find or a LinkedIn backup.

If any item fails, do a normal application plus a short note. A deep one takes 1 to 2 hours. Aim for 2 to 4 a week, best first.

## 1. Research their own words before drafting
- Read the posting, the application receipt, and every blog, case study, webinar, or resource it links. Save notes with quotes and links in the prep folder.
- Make a ranked list of problems the company says its customers or team face. Each item needs a source quote. Show the list before picking an idea.
- Check each problem against the company's own claims. Do not pitch a fix for something they say already works (pilots stall, if they claim a high pilot success rate). Separate an industry problem from this company's.
- Check whether they already do the idea. If their blog describes it, the pitch must say what is new, such as running it earlier, on older data, before go-live.

## 2. Pick recipients
- Prefer the real hiring manager. At a small company that is often the technical leader or a founder. If you cannot find the manager they would report to, pick people with a reason: someone who publicly invited outreach, plus the recruiter who owns hiring.
- When they work together, address them in one email ("Hi A and B,") instead of separate notes.
- Guess an email pattern only when a public signal supports it. Tell the candidate the addresses are unverified, and suggest LinkedIn as a backup.

## 3. Let the candidate shape the idea
Offer options, and use their hypothesis when they give one. Frame it as an idea, not a plan. They do not have inside data.

## 4. Structure (about 150 to 200 words)
1. **Role first.** "I'm X. I applied this week for <exact title>, and my resume is attached." Add a true reason you wrote to them, such as their post.
2. **What stood out.** One specific line about the company, with a source.
3. **Bridge.** One line from the candidate's background to the company's customers.
4. **Two proof bullets.** Use the exact scope, such as the team size for that title, not another role's.
5. **A question, then the idea.** A short question that tests the hypothesis ("Does much of the time go into ...?"), then the idea in one or two sentences, then the concrete benefit. Cite their blog if the idea builds on it.
6. **A light offer.** "If that's useful, I'd love to help build it."
7. **The ask.** "I'd love to chat about the role."

Subject: `<Role title>: my application and an idea for <their outcome>`. Clear. Avoid "plan", "requesting", and hype.

## 5. Review before the final draft
- Drop overclaims. Say "helps map" rather than "discovers". Drop a promise you cannot check, such as "payback in weeks", and say "prove value early" instead.
- Each idea is one connected thing, not two projects.
- Wording should be clear to the reader. For example, their word "firm" versus "client", and what "go-live" means.
- List likely pushback, with a one-line answer for each, so they are ready to reply.
- Show the whole email after each round of edits.

## 6. Gmail draft
- Only after the host-approved draft flow and the user's go, create the Gmail draft, then attach the resume with UploadFile to the draftId. Putting the file in base64 inside create_draft fails.
- Do not call update_draft once a draft has an attachment. Create a new draft, attach the file, and delete the old draft, so only one draft exists.
- The candidate sends. Once they say it is sent, log the recipients, the subject, the idea, and the date for follow-up. One follow-up after about a week.
