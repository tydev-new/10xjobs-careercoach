# Learn — the craft

Hints for the loop in `../SKILL.md`; the standard is `eval.md`, the file
shapes `schema.md`.

## The investment rule — two scopes

- **Track-scoped** (a class of roles = a criteria lane; the threshold is the loop's rule 1 — the same one the résumé standard uses). Durable: it compounds across every application in the lane.
  Train deep, ahead of need; coach's groundwork sensing counts these.
- **Role-scoped** (one pipeline row): demanded by a single JD.
  Perishable: train just-in-time when the role goes live (application
  committed or interview scheduled), to conversational credibility.  Promotion and retirement: `schema.md § Status lifecycle`.

## Mapping — getting there

Read the track's jd-analyses and `criteria.md § Targets`; one row per
topic per lane, scope and source per row. No jd-analyses yet → derive
from the track definition, label `[inferred]`, upgrade silently as real
ones land — mapping never blocks on a search having run. Assess in
scope order: track rows first (with only one JD in a lane, the track's
general knowledge IS the target, not that role's specifics); a
role-scoped row when its role goes live, not before.

## Courses — two types, one machinery

- **Domain-knowledge course** (learn's own): triggered by a Map gap going
  live — scoped by its row (scope + depth). The checklist is the topic's
  conversational canon: concepts, vocabulary, architectures, tradeoffs
  at the needed depth.
- **Interview-format course**: triggered by a prep brief's Format
  Discovery ("system design round at X") or the candidate naming a weak
  format — scoped to THAT variant (a Google-style system design and a
  Series-B technical deep-dive are different curricula). Generic "teach
  me system design" → ask which interview it's for; truly exploratory →
  scope to the band and function. Format courses are drilled by
  PRACTICE, where scored reps live.

**Building** — dispatch research across the public canon: current
best-practice guides, the standard frameworks, company-variant
write-ups, worked examples, commonly-reported questions. Tier every
source (evaluate's rules: cite / label / say-unknown). Write the seven
sections in `schema.md`. When research comes back thin (the loop's rule 3), the candidate is often the best source for the unknowns: "what do you know about the panel?"

**Refresh** on request, when reality contradicts the course, or when a
prep brief's format discovery contradicts its variant assumptions;
courses carry their research date.

## Training — reps against the checklist

Present a problem → the candidate works it aloud → score each
checklist dimension covered / partial / missed, plus the communication
dimensions (thinking-aloud structure, clarifying questions, tradeoff
articulation) → feedback names the MISSED canonical dimensions first
(coverage gaps are cheap points). The checklist makes progress
measurable ("5/7 dimensions unprompted, up from 3/7"). Every rep lands
in `## Assessment log`; format-course reps that practice scores land in
`practice-log.md` too.

## Saying the honest thing — the phrasings

Withheld: "strong in dbt". Said instead: "hands-on with dbt patterns, tested against the production canon on <date>". A gap, where it weakens something: "the payments gap is frameable, and 3 hours of study makes it defensible" — with the just-in-time plan attached.

## Proposing a new pattern

When a course structure or a quiz form proves itself across two topics,
write it as a dated line at the end of the course it came from — name
the two. A pattern is never self-adopted; a human promotes it into this
file.
