# Cover letter — Structural Engineer, Coldwater Bridge Group

Dear Coldwater team,

I'm applying for the Structural Engineer opening on your rehabilitation
practice. Bridge rehab is where I want to spend the next decade, and your
group's inspection-driven approach is the version of it I respect.

My background splits between transit and buildings. At Bayline Transit
Authority I spent five years on station structures and platform canopies
over live track, coordinating structural scope with track, systems, and
utilities before anything went to bid. At Calder & Moss I've been the
design lead on two mid-rise concrete-core buildings in downtown Oakland and
spent three years in construction support on the Harbor Bridge seismic
retrofit under Dana Whitfield's team. Construction support is where I
learned to read a structure the way an inspector does — corrosion first,
paperwork second, analysis third.

I'd describe myself as a detail-oriented team player: I sweat the small
stuff, and I route it past the people who have to build it before it ships.
On the Harbor Bridge work that habit meant walking the falsework with the
contractor's superintendent every Friday, which caught two clearance
problems while they were still erasable.

What draws me to Coldwater specifically is the Kestrel Slough crossing.
Load-rating a sixty-year-old riveted truss while keeping it in service is
exactly the kind of problem I want on my desk — equal parts archaeology and
engineering. I'd bring patience for the archives and skepticism for the
as-builts.

I hold PE licenses in California and Oregon, and I can share calc packages,
references, or job-site stories on request. I'd welcome a conversation
about the rehabilitation bench and where I could be useful on it.

Thank you for the consideration.

Tomás Rivera, PE
