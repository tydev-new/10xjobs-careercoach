# Resume summary from the job

## Goal
Make the strongest case for this role in words a reader can follow without the job text. The job chooses the evidence. It is not an outline they must rebuild.

## Process
1. Read the captured job text, profile, approved claims, and current resume. In private, map the top 3 to 5 duties or requirements to the strongest approved evidence. Keep that map in prep or review notes, not as public labels. Flag a must-have you cannot support.
2. Write a short introduction in full sentences, usually 3 or 4 bullets. Start with what they build or own. Explain an unfamiliar product before you rely on its name. Name the employer and role where that helps.
3. Show what they owned, how it worked, and what changed. Use the strongest approved numbers. Do not cram every metric into the first bullet. One idea per bullet.
4. When the employer, product, or kind of system changes, say what the work was. Do not treat earlier infrastructure work as AI or security work. Do not write a disclaimer such as "not LLM or security". Describe the real work.
5. Keep a jargon word only when it adds something, and give context. No short label on every bullet. No generic strengths list, keyword dump, unexplained product name, search-note voice, job paraphrase used as a label, or a second strengths block.
6. A location or work-authorization line, if useful, goes at the end. It does not count as fit.
7. Read the summary first without the job. A reader should see what they build, what the product does, what they owned, and what improved. Then check the private map. If a strong reason dropped, fix the story or put the proof in experience. Do not add a label or a fifth bullet just to tick a box.
8. Run the current mechanical precheck and the separate combined review from the apply workflow. This skill does not replace the claims check, best-candidate notes, rejection reasons, a fresh review, or approval rules.

## Boundaries
Separate nearby work from work they did. Do not add a claim or change a base resume without approval. A dry run is a preview until they say to apply it. Do not submit an application as part of writing the summary.
